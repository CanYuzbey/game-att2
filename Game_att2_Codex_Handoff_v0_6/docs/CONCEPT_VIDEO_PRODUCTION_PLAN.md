# Game att2 - Concept Video Production Plan

Status date: 2026-08-24

Status: **APPROVED COMMUNICATION DIRECTION; PAPER-ONLY PRODUCTION PLAN. NO VIDEO
ASSET, PLAYABLE DEMO, ENGINE RUNTIME, FINAL ART, FUN, MARKET DEMAND, OR INVESTOR
READINESS IS VERIFIED BY THIS DOCUMENT.**

## Purpose and relationship to the demo

`APPROVED DIRECTION`: the first near-term communication artifact is a short concept
video that explains the intended Underground City sample, its combat/body dynamics,
possible characters, and world promise before full game production begins. It is for
early discussion with friends, possible collaborators, and potential investors. The
bounded playable mini-game remains the longer-term gameplay-proof track and continues
under its existing D0 and explicit runtime-gate contract.

The video is not presented as captured gameplay while no playable new-demo build
exists. Until real capture replaces a shot, the persistent disclosure is:

> Pre-production visualization - not captured from a playable build.

The video may establish a coherent vision and reveal comprehension problems. It
cannot establish control feel, combat appeal, accessibility, retention, market
demand, production feasibility, or investor readiness. A later publisher/investor
package may also require a playable build, team, schedule, budget, funding request,
and financial roadmap. Current official examples include [Shueisha Games' pitch
process](https://shueisha-games.com/en/pitch/) and [Devolver Digital's pitch
requirements](https://pitch.devolverdigital.com/).

## Intended deliverables

`WORKING HYPOTHESIS`:

- one `2:00-2:30` master `Gameplay Vision / Concept Previsualization` film;
- one `30-45` second cutdown derived from the same master timeline;
- Turkish and English subtitles;
- a textless/no-voice export for later localization and recutting;
- one dated truth matrix, shot list, asset-rights ledger, and archived master;
- later replacement of concept shots with explicitly labelled real prototype capture
  where a verified playable artifact exists.

Exact duration, narration language, voice, title card, call to action, final music,
and delivery platform remain `OPEN` until the video brief is approved.

## Communication contract

After one viewing, an unbriefed viewer should be able to explain these intended
relationships without the designer supplying the answer:

1. The player becomes the weapon by rebuilding the body.
2. Cards declare techniques; the current body determines their physical legality.
3. Killing an opponent yields Blood and closes that opponent's limb route for the
   day; accepting living surrender yields the agreed limb and no kill-Blood reward.
4. Block spends another body part, Parry protects and reduces Will, and Red attacks
   require Evade.
5. Grafting creates a visible capability gain and drawback carried into a later
   pressure.
6. Death resets the same day and flesh while bounded Memory/Brain progress preserves
   an inspectable learned advantage.

The body/card/reward/defense relationships and same-day reset structure have paper
authority at the direction level. The exact first graft, Brain/Memory payload,
characters, factions, boss, theme, and art direction remain open or working
hypotheses and must be labelled `CONCEPT VARIANT` inside the truth matrix and review
copies.

## Truth matrix

Before storyboard approval, every shot receives one row:

`CONCEPT VARIANT` means an open or provisional choice has been selected only so the
film can depict one coherent possibility; it remains non-canon. `VERIFIED PROTOTYPE
CAPTURE` may be used only with the exact named build and fresh capture evidence.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| `ShotID` | Stable ID such as `S030` |
| `Claim` | What the viewer is expected to believe |
| `Authority` | `PAPER RULE`, `APPROVED DIRECTION`, `WORKING HYPOTHESIS`, `OPEN`, or `CONCEPT VARIANT` |
| `Source` | Exact living-document section |
| `Visual proof` | The observable cause and result shown in the shot |
| `Disclosure` | Previsualization, concept variant, or verified prototype capture |
| `Asset provenance` | Author, tool/source, license, and modification record |
| `Replacement trigger` | What verified build/capture would replace the mock shot |

No shot may turn an `OPEN` choice into apparent canon merely because the asset is
polished. Generated images or video must carry the same provenance and authority
classification as authored assets.

## Production stages and gates

### V0 - brief and claim lock

Create one page defining audience, one-sentence promise, desired response, duration
comparison, language, call to action, prohibited claims, and truth matrix.

Continue when every planned claim has an authority label and no scene implies
captured gameplay.

### V1 - script, storyboard, and animatic

Create `12-16` rough shots. Assemble stills, temporary narration, temporary sound,
and disclosure into a full-length animatic before detailed art.

Continue when a small cold-viewer check can explain the body/card relationship,
kill-versus-surrender split, graft consequence, and reset promise without coaching.
Failure here changes the script/edit, not asset fidelity.

### V2 - visual target

Finish only three representative hero frames first:

1. captive Guard concession;
2. combat/reward dilemma;
3. graft consequence or gate-boss pressure.

They compare silhouette, camera, palette, modular-body readability, card/UI language,
and the non-realistic graphic treatment of bodily harm. Continue only after one
coherent visual direction is selected; this selection does not approve the same art
direction for the final game.

### V3 - bounded shot production

Produce only assets visible in the approved shot list. Reuse modular bodies, fixed
camera layouts, environment modules, animation cycles, and UI components. Do not
build a general game system, full faction roster, broad world, final cinematic, or
production-quality inventory solely for the film.

### V4 - edit, sound, disclosure, and cold review

Complete picture lock, UI/motion graphics, color, sound, subtitles, rights review,
and master/cutdown exports. Run the comprehension questions below and retain raw
answers, prior exposure, and facilitator intervention. This is communication
evidence, not gameplay or market evidence.

## Minimal program chain

The default chain deliberately avoids duplicate tools:

```text
Markdown/CSV planning
-> Krita concept and storyboard
-> Blender graybox, modular 3D, camera, animation, PNG sequences
-> optional Figma UI source files
-> DaVinci Resolve animatic, edit, Fusion UI motion, Fairlight audio, color, captions
-> OBS capture only after a real prototype exists
```

### Markdown and CSV - control surface

Use repository text files for the brief, script, truth matrix, shot list, and asset
rights. A shot row owns duration, purpose, authority, visual, audio, source asset,
version, and review status. Text remains diffable and does not require a new planning
service.

### Krita - concept and storyboard

Use Krita for character silhouettes, environment sketches, modular-limb concepts,
card art, storyboard panels, and bounded frame-by-frame effects. Keep editable `.kra`
sources and export numbered `1920x1080` PNG panels such as `S030_v002.png`. Resolve
timing and silhouette before texture detail.

Official reference: <https://krita.org/en/features/>

### Blender - main visual production

Use Blender for the Underground City graybox, modular low-poly bodies, rigging,
camera blocking, animation, lighting, and render. Keep Head, Torso, Left Arm, Right
Arm, Legs, and Core as inspectable modules attached to one root so graft/loss shots
remain consistent with the six-slot paper body.

Start with colored primitives and low-quality preview renders. Upgrade only shots
that survive the animatic. Render image sequences rather than one movie so a failed
frame or changed shot can be replaced without rerendering the entire film.

Initial interchange comparison:

- `1920x1080`, `30 fps`;
- preview in Eevee/Workbench as appropriate;
- numbered PNG sequence for ordinary shots;
- transparent PNG only where UI/compositing requires alpha;
- `.blend` source per reusable scene or bounded shot group, not one uncontrolled
  all-purpose file.

Official references: <https://www.blender.org/features/> and
<https://www.blender.org/features/story-artist/>.

### Figma - optional UI source

Add Figma only when the card/HUD system needs reusable components and variants.
Create one card component with `Ready`, `Dormant`, `Invalid`, and `Selected` states;
separate Blood, Mana, Will, body target, Yellow, and Red components. Export SVG for
vector icons or transparent PNG for raster composition. Animate the final presentation
in Resolve Fusion or Blender, not in a second competing edit timeline.

For the first animatic, simple Krita or Resolve shapes are sufficient; Figma is not a
gate.

### DaVinci Resolve - animatic and finish

Use one Resolve project with separate timelines for animatic, master, and cutdown.
Organize media into Storyboard, Blender Preview, Final Renders, UI, Voice, SFX, Music,
and Exports bins.

- `Edit`: storyboard timing, rough cut, picture lock, and cutdown;
- `Fusion`: Blood/Will changes, target markers, card states, threat cues, disclosure,
  and titles;
- `Color`: visual continuity across shots;
- `Fairlight`: voice, dialogue, combat/body SFX, ambience, music, and final mix;
- `Deliver`: review copies, master, textless, subtitles, and web exports.

Keep dialogue/voice, combat/body SFX, ambience, and music on separate tracks. Use WAV
`48 kHz / 24-bit` source audio where possible. Yellow/Red cues need distinct non-color
shape/animation/audio language. Do not use unlicensed temporary music in any shared
or pitch export.

Initial delivery comparison:

- review/web: H.264 MP4, `1920x1080`, `30 fps`, stereo audio;
- archive: DNxHR master plus separate WAV mix and `.srt` subtitles;
- textless and no-voice variants from the same locked timeline.

Official reference: <https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve>.

### OBS Studio - later real gameplay capture

Do not use OBS to disguise a mock-up as gameplay. Once a verified prototype exists,
capture the exact named build at `60 fps`, keep microphone/game audio separable, and
record the build identifier and capture route. Real capture may replace matching
concept shots; it does not retroactively verify unreplaced scenes.

Official reference: <https://obsproject.com/>.

### Versioning and large files

Keep planning text and lightweight source metadata in ordinary Git. Use Git LFS or a
separately backed-up media store only after deciding ownership and storage limits for
large `.blend`, `.kra`, audio, image-sequence, and master-video files. Never commit
render cache, proxies, generated waveform data, Resolve cache, or disposable previews.

Official reference: <https://git-lfs.com/>.

media through the game-design tree:

```text

|-- planning/
|-- art/
|-- blender/
|-- audio/
|-- resolve/
`-- exports/
```

Suggested shot naming is `<ShotID>_<purpose>_v###`, for example
`S030_body_block_v004.blend`. Published exports include a date and revision. Cache and
render directories are generated locally and ignored.

This document creates no asset directory yet; it only defines the proposed boundary.

## Character and world scope for the film

Use at most four foreground functions before expanding the roster:

- the captive/rebuilding protagonist;
- the Guard whose pre-existing duty and need motivate a disclosed concession;
- one living opponent/graft relationship that demonstrates the reward split;
- the gate boss whose goal or duty guards the exit.

A Grafter or a few faction silhouettes may appear only when they clarify the body
economy or procedure. Placeholder faction families from the living ledger remain
concept directions, not named canon. Each consequential foreground actor still needs
a pre-existing Goal, Need, Red Line, and disclosed Claim; visual polish cannot replace
motivation.

## Review questions and interpretation

After one uninterrupted viewing, ask without explaining the mechanics first:

1. What does the player repeatedly do?
2. What determines whether a card can be used?
3. What is the difference between killing and accepting surrender?
4. What changed after the graft, including one drawback?
5. What persists after death?
6. Which moment made you want to see or play more, and why?
7. Which moment looked like real captured gameplay?

Question 7 is a disclosure audit: if a viewer mistakes previsualization for captured
gameplay, revise labelling or presentation. Record exact answers rather than reducing
the session to "liked/disliked." Friends and collaborators are a convenience sample;
their reaction can revise communication but cannot establish general market demand.

## Parallel demo boundary

The video and playable-demo tracks share the five living design documents and truth
matrix but produce different evidence:

| Track | May establish | Cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Concept video | Vision coherence, intended causal loop, early comprehension problems, visual/presentation direction | Control feel, rule fidelity in runtime, combat appeal, accessibility, build feasibility |
| Playable demo | Named build behavior, real input, state mutation, comprehension and bounded human-play observations | Broad market demand, retention, investor readiness |

Video work may not invent a final rule to unblock a shot. Demo work may not treat a
polished shot as a runtime specification or passing test. A weekly truth review should
identify any video promise that no longer matches the paper contract; the shot is
relabelled, revised, or removed.
