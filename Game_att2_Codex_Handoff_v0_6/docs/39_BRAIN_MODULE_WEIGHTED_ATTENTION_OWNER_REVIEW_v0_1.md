# Game att2 - Brain Module and Weighted Attention Slots

Status date: 2026-08-21

Status: **OWNER-APPROVED PAPER DESIGN DIRECTION ONLY; NUMBERS, CONTENT, HUMAN
EVIDENCE, RUNTIME, CONFIGURATION, PERSISTENCE IMPLEMENTATION, AND FINAL UI NOT
APPROVED**

This document is the current design authority for the Brain Module, permanent Brain
Part progression, weighted Attention Slot selection, the body-card hand lifecycle,
and the Brain/inventory boundary. It supersedes document 29 where that document fixes
slot guarantees, three-to-five capacity as approved numbers, or free delayed
Reconsider. It supersedes document 34 only where that document requires an inventory
opportunity to occupy an Attention Slot. Document 34's inventory ownership, timing,
source, use, one-voluntary-inventory-action, and no-substitution rules remain binding.

Approval is paper-only. It does not authorize a runtime card system, save data, meta-
progression code, new card or Brain Part content, Head anatomy, Stun, Freeze, another
status, an extra Main action, enemy content, or production UI.

## 1. Owner-approved result

The Brain Module is the primary permanent roguelite progression layer. The body owns
which physical opportunities exist. The Brain controls which currently valid body-
card opportunities reach attention and, for rarer authored Brain Parts, may modify a
card played from a particular slot.

```text
permanently unlocked Brain Parts
-> one run-start Brain Part configuration
-> current usable body sources and their card instances
-> weighted, source-valid Attention Slot selection
-> persistent hand plus one optional Blood-paid redraw
-> Preparation/Main commitment and automatic reflex resolution
-> body consequences rebuild the eligible pool
```

More or stronger Brain development normally changes choice consistency and hand
behavior. It does not normally create physical capability or extra voluntary plays.

## 2. Terminology and ownership

- **Brain Module:** the complete permanent progression and card-selection system.
- **Attention Slot:** one cognitive position that holds one selected body-card
  instance.
- **Brain Part:** one rare, permanently unlocked component installed into one
  Attention Slot at run start.
- **Brain Part rule:** that slot's single non-stacking filter, weight, or authored
  modifier.
- **Body card:** a voluntary tactical opportunity generated by one exact usable
  physical source or declared set of sources.

Attention Slots do not add or replace any of the existing six body slots. Brain Parts
are not ordinary enemy anatomy and are not harvested from ordinary enemies. The exact
anatomical relationship between the Brain Module and Head remains deferred for the
later body-slot discussion.

## 3. Permanent Brain Part progression

1. Brain Parts are rare permanent unlocks.
2. Approved acquisition families are boss rewards, side-mission rewards, and other
   later-authored non-ordinary-enemy rewards.
3. A Brain Part found during a run cannot be installed during that run.
4. It becomes configurable after the run ends and persists through later deaths.
5. At the beginning of a run, the player assigns at most one unlocked Brain Part to
   each available Attention Slot.
6. The chosen configuration is locked for that run.
7. The player may rearrange unlocked Brain Parts before a later run.
8. Brain Parts do not stack within one slot. Changing a slot rule means replacing the
   installed Brain Part before the run.

Exact reward conditions, persistence data format, fiction, acquisition UI, and
slot-unlock costs are implementation/content details. Once unlocked, a Brain Part is
not lost to a failed run or player death. No save or progression implementation is
approved here.

## 4. Body-card pool

The selection pool is rebuilt only from current source-supported opportunities.

- One body part may generate multiple distinct cards.
- Multiple distinct cards from the same source may occupy different Attention Slots
  at the same time.
- Shared physical commitment remains binding: using or reserving that source may make
  its other cards Dormant or Invalid.
- Two separate but identical physical sources create separately identified card
  instances. For example, left- and right-arm instances may share a name while
  retaining different exact source ownership.
- One card instance cannot occupy more than one slot simultaneously.
- Cards may declare more than one category tag, such as both `ATTACK` and `DEFENCE`.
- A card's tags never waive its timing, range, target, source, cost, risk, or physical
  compatibility.
- Disabled, Ruined, Severed, Missing, or otherwise Offline sources contribute no
  executable cards.
- A temporarily blocked source may leave a held card Dormant when its opportunity
  still exists; permanent loss makes the card Invalid.

The Brain never fabricates a capability, changes an unusable source into a usable
one, or selects a hidden substitute source.

## 5. Weighted Attention Slot selection

The ordinary Brain uses probabilistic selection, not composition guarantees.

- There is no guaranteed Attack card.
- There is no guaranteed Defence card.
- All-Attack, all-Defence, utility-heavy, or otherwise uneven hands remain possible
  when the usable body pool contains enough matching card instances.
- Flexible starting slots use later-tested baseline weights intended to support an
  average round without eliminating category droughts.
- A Brain Part may change a slot's weights or apply a hard filter such as
  `ATTACK_ONLY` or `DEFENCE_ONLY`.
- A hard-filtered slot shades when no remaining eligible instance satisfies it.
- A shaded slot may reactivate at a later Decision Refresh when pool state changes.

The paper selector order is:

```text
enumerate unique source-owned card instances
-> remove permanently impossible opportunities
-> calculate current profiles, tags, costs, risk, and legality
-> resolve hard-filtered slots before flexible slots
-> fill remaining slots by declared weighted sampling without replacement
-> emit a seeded, inspectable selection record
```

Resolving constrained slots first prevents a flexible slot from accidentally taking
the only instance that can satisfy a hard-filtered slot. If multiple valid assignments
remain, injected seeded randomness may choose among them and must record the choice.

## 6. Slot capacity and power boundary

No final starting count, developed count, or cap is approved. The earlier three-slot
and five-slot values remain comparison fixtures because they bound useful small-hand
tests; they are not final progression numbers. Slot count, category weights, redraw
cost, card content, and Brain Part strength must be evaluated together because each
changes consistency and can dominate body progression.

Ordinary Brain Parts primarily modify:

- category or tag probability;
- source or card-family probability;
- hard category filtering;
- later-approved repetition or refresh behavior.

Rarer Brain Parts may modify a card played from their slot. The modifier belongs to
the slot, not permanently to the card. The same card drawn into another slot does not
retain it. Execution modifiers must remain body-dependent and may not silently bypass
source legality, physical commitment, cost, range, target, or consequence rules.

Illustrations such as `+10% Attack`, `Attack cards from this slot deal +10%`, or
`Defence cards from this slot apply Freeze` describe possible design families only.
They do not approve a value, Freeze, another status, or production content.

## 7. Persistent hand and Decision Refresh

The first approved comparison baseline is a persistent hand with controlled redraw:

1. Draw the opening hand at combat start.
2. Unused, still-valid cards remain in their slots.
3. A played card becomes Spent and does not refill immediately.
4. Spent slots refill at the next Decision Refresh.
5. If a source becomes permanently unusable during an exchange, its dependent card
   instance disappears immediately.
6. Replacement waits until the next Decision Refresh; no source invalidation or card
   play creates a mid-exchange draw.
7. A slot with no eligible match remains visibly shaded.
8. Temporary source, posture, range, or commitment blocks may leave a card Dormant
   with a visible reason.

Full-round redraw and strict played-card-only refill remain required comparison
variants. They are not coequal approved defaults.

## 8. Once-per-round Blood-paid redraw

Once per round, before the actor locks a commitment, the player may choose one unused
card for immediate reconsideration.

```text
selected unused card and slot
-> validate at least one alternative eligible instance for that slot
-> if none exists, disable redraw and spend nothing
-> preview and pay the authored Blood cost
-> apply normal Blood consequences
-> remove the selected instance from this redraw's candidate set when another exists
-> select and display the replacement immediately
```

The replacement may be strategically worse. Redraw does not consume Preparation or
Main and cannot be repeated that round unless a later explicit rare Brain Part says
otherwise. Exact Blood cost and any future cost scaling remain deferred. Because
Blood is life, currency, and fuel, the eventual preview must expose any Panic Pulse,
Blood-0, or other approved consequence before payment.

This replaces document 29's free, delayed Reconsider rule.

## 9. Inventory boundary

Inventory is not part of the ordinary Brain hand.

- Owned items and tools are inspected and selected from inventory, not randomly drawn
  into Attention Slots.
- Inventory opportunities do not compete with body cards for Attention Slot capacity.
- Inventory continues to own quantities, uses, expiry, item state, required sources,
  and loss.
- Activated inventory opportunities retain their authored Preparation or Main timing.
- At most one voluntary inventory-origin action may execute per actor per round.
- A Preparation inventory action therefore cannot be followed by a second voluntary
  inventory Main in the same round.
- Exact tools, grips, sources, targets, and item instances remain reserved after lock;
  no silent substitution is allowed.
- Ordinary execution-time uses are deducted only when execution begins.
- Passive equipment and automatic Reflexive Defence remain outside the voluntary
  inventory-action limit.
- State-required actions remain directly surfaced from real state and ownership; they
  do not require an Attention Slot and do not invent an item or bonus play.

A rare Brain Part may explicitly affect an inventory or other non-body interaction.
Such an exception does not place inventory into the ordinary Brain hand and must name
the exact rule it modifies.

This supersedes document 34's Readied Inventory slot requirement while preserving its
origin, ownership, timing, source, use, one-action, passive/active, signature-override,
and evidence contracts.

## 10. Reflex, extra-play, and Head boundaries

- Reflex defence remains automatically surfaced from the incoming action and current
  build. It is not drawn, held, or manually played from an Attention Slot.
- A rare Brain Part may explicitly modify a reflex profile, but ordinary Brain
  progression does not create another reflex event or guarantee a counter.
- Very rare Brain Parts may eventually grant another card play in a turn. This
  possibility is acknowledged but completely deferred: no extra Preparation, Main,
  timing class, value, or content is approved.
- Ordinary Head or body damage cannot permanently destroy unlocked Brain Parts.
- A later authored attack may temporarily Stun or disrupt Brain operation. No Stun
  rule, duration, slot consequence, attack, or content is approved here.
- Baseline Head damage does not disable the whole hand or change Attention Slot
  capacity by implication.

## 11. Hidden enemy hands and symmetry

Future player and enemy implementations should share one internal Attention Slot and
source-legality engine.

- Enemy cards are generated from their usable physical sources.
- Ordinary enemies use fixed internal Brain profiles rather than permanent player
  progression.
- Special enemies may use unusual hidden weights or Brain rules when explicitly
  authored.
- Enemy hands and Brain Parts are not shown to the player.
- The player sees approved visible intent and learns hidden tendencies through action
  results and repeated observation.
- Enemy AI may rank legal cards in its hidden hand, but cannot select a card absent
  from that hand or act from an unusable source.
- No enemy-hand UI is required.

This shared internal model is the approved implementation direction because it avoids
parallel player-card and enemy-action legality systems while preserving hidden enemy
information. It remains runtime-gated.

## 11A. Owner-delegated collision resolutions

On 2026-08-21 the owner asked Codex to compare the current systems, resolve collisions,
and use logic for unanswered Brain details. The following paper-only interpretations
close reversible gaps without approving numbers, content, or runtime:

- The approved baseline has no separate collectible technique deck or active-deck
  editor. The current body defines the card-instance pool; Brain configuration shapes
  access. A conventional deck layer requires a later justification and approval.
- A multi-tag card is one instance. It may satisfy any hard filter matching at least
  one tag, but it is never duplicated or counted twice merely because two tags match.
- A card rejected through the paid redraw is excluded only from that redraw's
  candidate set when an alternative exists. It is not destroyed and may return at a
  later Decision Refresh if still source-valid.
- `Run start` and `run end` are local Brain-configuration boundaries only. They do not
  define map topology, room cadence, checkpoints, body retention, or the fiction of
  death and restart.
- Brain Part ownership is separate from body anatomy, inventory ownership, and any
  future technique knowledge. Only Brain Parts are approved as permanent progression
  here.
- Slot-local execution modifiers follow the slot when it refills; they never attach
  permanently to a discarded, Spent, Invalid, or redrawn card instance.
- If no body card can satisfy any open slot, the slots shade. Direct inventory,
  state-required actions, and automatic reflexes remain available only when their own
  rules make them legal; none fabricates a body card.

These choices are reversible at paper level. They remove duplicate progression and
ambiguous instance behavior while preserving body-as-build.

## 13. Minimum paper comparisons

Compare at least:

- candidate starting and developed slot counts, including the historical three/five
  fixtures;
- persistent plus paid redraw, full-round redraw, and strict refill-after-use;
- balanced, aggressive, defensive, wounded, and source-starved bodies;
- flexible, weighted, and hard-filtered Brain Parts;
- multiple cards from one source and identical cards from two exact sources;
- player-visible and enemy-hidden hands;
- affordable, unaffordable, lethal, unavailable, and strategically poor redraws.

Record:

- category and exact-source distribution;
- Attack/Defence drought length;
- all-one-category hand frequency;
- shaded-slot frequency and duration;
- repeated-card frequency;
- redraw use, Blood cost, and resulting Blood consequences;
- source-invalid selections prevented;
- slot-count and Brain-Part dominance over body upgrades;
- deterministic seed/selection traces;
- human explanation of source, slot effect, cost, and disabled reason.

Simulation may establish probability, legality, determinism, reachable states, and
exploit resistance. It cannot establish fun, fairness, comprehension, frustration,
accessibility, or replay desire.

## 14. Continue, revise, and kill criteria

Continue if changing the body materially changes the pool; changing Brain Parts
materially changes tendencies; imperfect hands occur without prolonged helplessness;
source loss always removes capability; paid redraw creates a meaningful Blood choice;
and enemy behavior remains inferable from visible intent and results.

Revise if slot count is always the strongest permanent upgrade, hard-filtered slots
shade excessively, redraw is automatically optimal, persistent hands repeatedly trap
the player, Brain modifiers dominate the supplying body, or hidden enemy hands make
legal results feel arbitrary.

Kill or substantially redesign the package if the Brain fabricates physical actions,
guarantees perfect counters, routinely grants extra Main actions, or turns body parts
into cosmetic card flavor.

## 17. Explicit deferrals and production gate

Deferred:

- all final slot counts, caps, weights, costs, percentages, status payloads, and Brain
  Part/card content;
- Head anatomy, temporary Stun/disruption, rare reflex modifiers, and rare extra-card
  plays;
- boss/side-mission reward content and acquisition fiction;
- save data, permanent progression implementation, runtime/config migration, tests,
  and implementation planning;
- final hand, inventory, enemy-intent, accessibility, animation, audio, and UI design;
- balance, fun, fairness, comprehension, accessibility, market, or replay claims;
- Unity, Encounter 3 runtime, new runtime enemies, and production integration.

This approval does not change the existing simulator, the seven approved scenarios,
the current Focus/Fast-item/Main runtime sequence, or the active dependency-ordered
product gate of mental defeat, surrender, and mercy.
