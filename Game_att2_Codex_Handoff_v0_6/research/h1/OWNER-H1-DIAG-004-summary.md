## Owner post-session interpretation

The owner reported that the session did not validate reflex usage adequately. The
task amounted to testing whether one second could be estimated and did not represent
the intended range of combat adaptations.

The owner expects reflex interactions to be classified and tested separately. Most
readable physical actions may use a click at the right time, with action- and state-
dependent timing, while other responses may require directional drag/movement or
multiple meaningful clicks. The diagnostic must explain and display how different
input timings and forms change the actual state consequence.

This qualitative feedback is owner-diagnostic evidence, not external-player evidence.
It supports revising the instrument and design taxonomy; it does not validate any
specific control family, threshold, damage value, accessibility profile, or fun claim.

The owner subsequently resolved input ownership: the rival's attack will normally
define compatible routes, the expected input type, and baseline difficulty, while the
player may direct the input type by choosing another legal response route. Some
attacks are intended to be answered by easy movements and others by hard ones. This
is a design decision informed by the diagnostic, not an experience claim.

The owner also approved a symmetric early/late curve for ordinary timed interactions,
with action-defined asymmetry available to specifically defined harder attacks when
the different consequences are visible. This remains design direction rather than a
validated timing or balance result.

The owner further directed that repeated Block use should be the primary source of
shrinking later Block windows, with low Blood visibly amplifying the pressure. The
intended purpose is to prevent an indefinite defensive loop without meaningful
counterpressure. Explicit impairment still changes legality or effectiveness. This
is owner design direction; fun, viable numeric pressure, and reset behavior remain
unvalidated.
